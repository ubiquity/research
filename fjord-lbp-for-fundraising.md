# Fjord LBP For Fundraising

Prepared for `ubiquity/research#143`.

## Executive Recommendation

Fjord Foundry is viable as a fundraising path only if Ubiquity treats it as a
structured public distribution campaign, not as a quick capital shortcut.

Recommendation:

1. Do a two-week readiness sprint before committing to any public sale.
2. Ask Fjord and the known curators/partners for direct feedback on whether the
   Ubiquity token story is strong enough for a partnered/curated sale.
3. Use a small, controlled LBP only after tokenomics, jurisdiction gating,
   market-making, post-sale liquidity, and communications are ready.
4. Do not launch an LBP just because OLAS and Merit Circle had attractive
   headline outcomes. Their outcomes came from narrative, timing, community, and
   prior ecosystem credibility, not the LBP mechanism alone.

The best near-term decision is:

> Start the Fjord diligence process now, but make launch approval conditional on
> narrative readiness, compliance review, and partner validation.

## Why Fjord Is Worth Diligence

Fjord's current public positioning fits the problem in the issue: transparent
on-chain capital formation and community-backed token launches.

Signals:

- Fjord publicly reports more than `100k` participants, more than `$1B` raised,
  and more than `$1.5B` traded volume.
- Fjord supports permissionless community sales and partnered sales.
- Fjord lists Liquidity Bootstrapping Pools as a core sale model for market-led
  price discovery.
- Fjord's public case studies include Autonolas and Merit Circle:
  - Autonolas: `$547k` raised, `99%` sold, current FDV shown as `$1.20bn`.
  - Merit Circle: `$105m` raised, `55%` sold, current FDV shown as `$523m`.

## What The OLAS Example Actually Shows

OLAS is the most relevant public comparison because it used Fjord explicitly for
a seven-day LBP with a wide-distribution narrative.

Key facts from OLAS's own launch post:

- The sale used Fjord Foundry.
- The stated goal was broad distribution at a market-determined cost.
- Initial pool balances were `15m OLAS` and `50k USDC`.
- OLAS described the LBP as a Balancer smart pool where prices dynamically
  adjust based on weights and balances.
- OLAS explicitly framed the mechanism as a way to reduce sharp price spikes,
  broader distribution, and bot/early-buyer manipulation.
- OLAS noted that Fjord could block access from restricted jurisdictions,
  including the United States.

Implication for Ubiquity:

The useful lesson is not "LBP creates high FDV." The useful lesson is that OLAS
had a clear protocol narrative and used the sale as a distribution event. If
Ubiquity cannot explain why a broad public holder base strengthens the protocol,
the LBP will look like a token sale instead of a community formation mechanism.

## What The Merit Circle Example Shows

Fjord highlights Merit Circle as a much larger outcome: `$105m` raised with
`55%` sold.

Implication for Ubiquity:

Merit Circle is not a realistic capital benchmark for Ubiquity unless the team
already has a comparably large community, partner set, and investor demand.
Merit Circle should be used for process questions:

- How did they sequence partner communications before launch?
- How did they choose sale size and token percentage?
- What market-making and post-sale liquidity plan did they use?
- How did they manage participant expectations?
- What legal/compliance steps created the slowest bottleneck?

## LBP Fit For Ubiquity

### Good Fit If

- The token has a clear utility story beyond fundraising.
- Ubiquity wants broad public distribution, not only treasury capital.
- There is enough existing community demand to create credible price discovery.
- The team can tolerate a market-clearing valuation below internal expectations.
- There is a post-sale plan for liquidity, communications, and holder support.
- Jurisdiction restrictions and legal disclosures are ready before marketing.

### Poor Fit If

- The main goal is a high headline valuation.
- Token utility is still unsettled.
- Ubiquity needs predictable capital more than market-led price discovery.
- The team is not ready to block restricted jurisdictions.
- No one owns post-sale liquidity and holder communications.
- A poor sale would damage future strategic fundraising.

## Recommended Sale Shape

This is a planning baseline, not final legal or financial advice.

Preferred route:

- Sale type: partnered or curated LBP, not a cold permissionless listing.
- Duration: 5 to 7 days.
- Primary objective: price discovery plus distribution.
- Secondary objective: expand the contributor/backer community.
- Token allocation: small enough to avoid over-distribution before token utility
  is battle-tested.
- Starting weight: steep enough to discourage first-block sniping.
- End weight: enough to let real demand clear without collapsing the sale.
- Initial stable liquidity: sized as a signal, not as the full desired raise.
- Max per-wallet or allowlist: only if Fjord supports the needed gating and it
  does not undermine the market-discovery objective.

Do not finalize parameters until Fjord and legal/compliance have reviewed the
sale constraints.

## Risk Register

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Securities/regulatory risk | Public token sales can trigger jurisdiction-specific obligations. | Get legal review before public marketing or sale creation. |
| U.S. and restricted-jurisdiction access | OLAS explicitly noted that Fjord may block certain jurisdictions. | Decide whether restricted access is acceptable before launch. |
| Narrative weakness | LBP demand depends on story, not only mechanics. | Prepare the token utility narrative before talking about raise targets. |
| Weak sale optics | A low raise or poor clearing price can hurt future fundraising. | Run partner diligence and soft demand checks first. |
| Whale concentration | Public sales can still concentrate if no controls exist. | Ask Fjord about wallet caps, allowlists, vesting, and anti-sybil options. |
| Post-sale liquidity | Holders expect liquidity after purchase. | Assign owner for liquidity plan before launch approval. |
| Operational load | A token sale requires support, announcements, claims, and incident response. | Build launch runbook and support rotation. |
| Tokenomics mismatch | Selling too much too early can weaken incentives. | Keep allocation conservative and align vesting with protocol milestones. |

## Diligence Questions For Fjord

Send these before any launch commitment:

1. What sale types are currently available for a project like Ubiquity?
2. What are the requirements for a partnered/curated sale?
3. What chain(s) and quote assets are recommended for the Ubiquity audience?
4. Can wallet caps, allowlists, or jurisdiction gates be configured?
5. What KYC/account verification is required for creators?
6. What participant restrictions apply by jurisdiction?
7. What fees apply to creators and participants?
8. What are the recommended weight schedules for a small-to-medium protocol
   sale?
9. What post-sale token claim and liquidity patterns are most common?
10. What launch support does Fjord or a curator provide?

## Diligence Questions For OLAS / Merit Circle / Curators

Ask Merit Circle:

- What was the single most important pre-launch preparation step?
- Did the LBP attract long-term holders or mostly short-term buyers?
- What would they change about allocation, duration, or communication?
- How did they manage post-sale liquidity and expectations?

Ask OLAS/Valory:

- Why did they choose `15m OLAS` and `50k USDC` as initial balances?
- How did they decide the 7-day duration?
- Which legal/jurisdiction steps took longest?
- Did the LBP improve contributor/community quality after launch?

Ask Coud + Grills:

- What makes a Fjord sale credible enough for their support?
- What Ubiquity narrative would be strongest or weakest?
- What FDV range would they consider defensible?
- What launch mistakes do they see most often?

## Two-Week Readiness Sprint

### Day 1-2: Decision Brief

- Assign one owner for fundraising readiness.
- Confirm the token's current utility, supply, vesting, and sale constraints.
- Decide whether Ubiquity wants price discovery, community distribution, or
  treasury funding as the primary objective.

### Day 3-4: Partner Feedback

- Contact Fjord.
- Contact Coud and Grills.
- Ask OLAS and Merit Circle for short experience notes.
- Record answers in a shared diligence document.

### Day 5-7: Tokenomics And Legal Gate

- Draft candidate sale parameters.
- Run legal/compliance review.
- Decide blocked jurisdictions and disclosure language.
- Decide whether the sale needs allowlist or wallet caps.

### Day 8-10: Communications

- Draft public sale announcement.
- Draft FAQ for participants.
- Draft "why public distribution matters" narrative.
- Draft incident/support runbook.

### Day 11-14: Go / No-Go

- Score readiness using the checklist below.
- Only proceed if all hard gates pass.
- If not ready, convert findings into smaller GitHub tasks and postpone launch.

## Go / No-Go Checklist

Hard gates:

- Legal review completed.
- Token utility narrative approved.
- Sale allocation approved.
- Jurisdiction gating plan approved.
- Post-sale liquidity owner assigned.
- Support/runbook owner assigned.
- Fjord or curator feedback received.
- Launch announcement and participant FAQ drafted.

Soft gates:

- At least three credible partners agree the narrative is strong.
- At least one previous Fjord participant/operator gives process feedback.
- Ubiquity can explain why public distribution strengthens the protocol.
- The team is comfortable with market-clearing price uncertainty.

No-go if:

- The sale is being used to force a high FDV.
- The team cannot answer basic token utility questions.
- The team has no post-sale liquidity plan.
- Legal review is incomplete.

## Bottom Line

Fjord should stay on the fundraising roadmap. It has a credible track record and
the right mechanism for broad public distribution. But Ubiquity should not
launch until the token story, legal posture, partner validation, and post-sale
operations are ready.

The next action is not "create sale." The next action is a two-week readiness
sprint that produces a go/no-go decision.
